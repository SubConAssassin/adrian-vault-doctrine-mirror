### 🔴 GEO — yesterday's finding is contradicted by a bigger study, and the resolution is a methodology lesson

Yesterday this log recorded, and built a tactic delta on: *"68% of AI citations come from third-party sources; only 32% from brand-owned"*, with the read that **off-site presence outranks on-site content as the GEO lever for SS.** **That recommendation should be held, and here is why.**

- **🔴 The largest attributable study points the opposite way. Yext analysed 6.8 million AI citations across ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity — 1.6 million queries per model, 20,820 unique citation domains — and found 86% come from brand-CONTROLLED sources**: first-party websites 2.9M citations (44%) and business listings 2.9M (42%). **Reviews and social 8%. Reddit and forums just 2%.** Per-model split: **Gemini favours websites (52.1%), OpenAI leans on listings (48.7%), Perplexity diversifies across MapQuest and TripAdvisor.** [CONFIRMED first-party press release with methodology — [Yext](https://www.yext.com/about/news-media/ai-citations-release), [Techstrong](https://techstrong.ai/articles/brands-control-most-ai-search-citations-new-study-finds/)]
- **🔴 BUT THE DATE IS THE CATCH, AND IT IS THE FOURTH CONSECUTIVE RUN THE MARKETING HALF HAS PRODUCED A FALSE-RECENCY ITEM. The Yext release is dated 9 October 2025 and the data was collected July–August 2025.** It surfaced under an August-2026-scoped search as though it were current. **It is a year old.** [CONFIRMED — press release date verified first-party]
- **🔴 AND THE 38% FIGURE IS AN AGGREGATOR ARTEFACT.** The search summary attributed *"only 38% of AI citations come from top-10 organic results"* to the Yext study. **I fetched the Yext release directly: it contains no organic top-10 overlap metric at all.** The number was stitched onto Yext's name by the aggregating page. **This log holds a 37.9%/38% top-10 overlap figure from two prior samples — that figure may still be right, but it is NOT corroborated by Yext, and anyone treating this as a third independent confirmation would be triple-counting.** [CONFIRMED by first-party absence]
- **🟢 THE ACTUAL RESOLUTION, and it is more useful than either number: the three studies are not measuring the same thing, so they do not conflict — they answer different questions.** Fetched directly, the source behind yesterday's 68% figure turns out to cite **Muck Rack (Dec 2025): 82% of AI citations are earned media, 94% non-paid, journalism 20–30%** — a **PR/journalism** query population. **Yext's population is location and local-business intent** (hence listings, MapQuest, TripAdvisor at 42%). **ConvertMate's "83% of AI Overview citations come from outside the organic top 10" is a fourth population again.** [CONFIRMED — [Omnibound](https://www.omnibound.ai/blog/generative-engine-optimization-statistics) as the aggregator, with its underlying attributions read out]
  - **→ The operative lesson: "where do AI citations come from" has no single answer, because the answer is determined by the query type. Local-intent queries cite listings; news-adjacent queries cite journalism; commercial-research queries cite something else again. A blended percentage across all of them is close to meaningless.**
  - **→ For SS specifically, the question that matters is not "brand-owned vs third-party in general" but "what does an AI cite when someone asks about a *coaching methodology* or a *transformation programme*" — and none of these four studies measures that population.** **That is an unanswered question, and it is the right one.**
